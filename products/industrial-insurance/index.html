Industrial Insurance (Industrial All Risks)

Industrial insurance covers the physical assets and operating income of a manufacturing plant or factory, usually through an Industrial All Risks (IAR) policy. IAR combines property damage (fire and allied perils on an all-risks basis), machinery breakdown, and business interruption in a single contract with high limits — the standard cover for a site with a total insurable value above ₹50 crore.

In short

  • What IAR bundles: all-risks property damage, machinery/plant breakdown, and business interruption following either — under one policy, one excess structure and one renewal.
  • Threshold: IAR is available for locations with a total sum insured of ₹50 crore or more; smaller units use Bharat Laghu or Sookshma Udyam Suraksha plus standalone machinery cover.
  • Valuation: buildings and plant on reinstatement value; stock at market value; BI on gross profit with an indemnity period matched to rebuild and re-commissioning time.
  • Companion covers: marine (inbound raw material / outbound finished goods), public & product liability, workmen's compensation, boiler & pressure plant, and group employee benefits.
  • Risk engineering: insurers survey the plant; improving fire protection and housekeeping directly lowers premium.

What is Industrial Insurance (Industrial All Risks)?

A manufacturing site concentrates enormous value — buildings, production lines, utilities, raw material and finished stock — and its income depends on that plant running. A single fire, explosion, flood or major machinery failure can halt production for months. Industrial insurance is designed to absorb exactly that kind of shock.

The Industrial All Risks policy is the market standard for larger plants. Instead of buying fire, machinery breakdown and business interruption separately, IAR provides them as one all-risks contract: it covers 'all risks' of physical loss or damage except what is specifically excluded, adds breakdown of plant and machinery, and pays the gross profit lost while the plant is down. Coverage

What industrial insurance covers — and what it doesn’t

A quick side-by-side so you know exactly where the policy helps.

Typically covered

  • All risks of sudden physical loss or damage to buildings, plant, machinery and stock (except listed exclusions)
  • Fire, lightning, explosion, storm, flood, earthquake shock, riot and impact
  • Sudden and unforeseen breakdown of plant and machinery — electrical, mechanical and pressure
  • Business interruption — loss of gross profit and standing charges following an insured property or machinery loss
  • Increased cost of working to keep production going after a loss
  • Debris removal, professional fees and, by extension, off-site utilities and infrastructure
  • Optional — marine, product liability, boiler explosion, terrorism, and machinery loss of profit

Usually excluded

  • Wear and tear, gradual deterioration, corrosion, erosion, scaling and cavitation
  • Faulty design, defective material or bad workmanship (the defective part itself)
  • Consumables, tools, dies, moulds and belts subject to frequent replacement
  • Loss discovered only at a stocktaking, and unexplained inventory shortage
  • War, nuclear risk, and pollution/contamination not resulting from an insured peril
  • Deliberate acts and operation of a machine known to be defective
  • Fines, penalties and consequential loss beyond the business-interruption cover

Exact inclusions and exclusions vary by insurer and plan — your BTW IMF advisor walks you through the wording before you buy.

How to choose the right industrial insurance

  1. Match the cover to the risk. Work out the worst realistic loss — a long hospital stay, a total-loss claim, an income gap — and buy enough industrial insurance to absorb it.
  2. Compare like for like. Premium is only meaningful next to the sum insured, sub-limits, waiting periods and exclusions. We line these up side by side.
  3. Check the insurer, not just the plan. Claim-settlement record, turnaround time and how the insurer treats disclosure disputes matter as much as price.
  4. Add only the riders you'll use. A few add-ons sharply improve real payouts; the rest just raise the premium.

Who needs industrial insurance?

Manufacturing companies, processing plants, warehouses attached to production, and industrial estates — particularly those with a total insurable value at a location of ₹50 crore or more, financed plant and machinery (lenders require cover), or contracts that oblige them to carry specified limits. Smaller workshops and units use the standardised Sookshma/Laghu products with add-on machinery and BI cover.

What affects your premium

  • Total sum insured and the split across building, plant, stock and BI gross profit
  • Occupancy — what is manufactured and the process hazard (e.g. chemicals, textiles, food)
  • Fire protection, housekeeping, and the insurer's risk-survey grading of the plant
  • Location — seismic and flood zones, exposure to neighbouring risks
  • Indemnity period chosen for business interruption
  • Claims history and the excess / deductible structure

FAQs

Industrial Insurance (Industrial All Risks) — frequently asked questions

What is an Industrial All Risks (IAR) policy?

IAR is a combined policy for large manufacturing sites that packages property damage on an 'all risks' basis, machinery breakdown, and business interruption into one contract. It replaces buying separate fire, machinery breakdown and loss-of-profit policies, and is available for locations with a total sum insured of ₹50 crore or more.

What is the difference between fire insurance and industrial insurance?

A fire policy covers a defined list of perils (fire, flood, riot, impact, etc.) affecting property. Industrial insurance, through IAR, covers 'all risks' of sudden physical damage — a wider trigger — and also builds in machinery breakdown and business interruption. It is the appropriate cover for a plant whose scale and complexity outgrow a standard fire policy.

Does industrial insurance cover machinery breakdown?

Yes. IAR includes sudden and unforeseen breakdown of plant and machinery — electrical, mechanical and pressure failures — as standard. Smaller units below the IAR threshold add a standalone Machinery Breakdown policy to their fire cover.

How is business interruption cover calculated for a factory?

It is based on your gross profit (turnover less specified variable costs) and an 'indemnity period' — the realistic maximum time to rebuild the plant, re-install machinery, re-commission and recover market share. Setting the indemnity period too short is a common and costly error; 18–36 months is typical for heavy plant.

Will the insurer inspect the plant before offering cover?

For any significant industrial risk, yes. The insurer's risk engineers survey the site and grade its fire protection, housekeeping, layout and process controls. The survey drives the premium and produces recommendations; acting on them reduces cost and improves claims outcomes.

Is industrial insurance mandatory?

The property programme itself is not legally mandatory, but lenders financing plant and machinery require it, and specific statutory covers apply to industry — workmen's compensation or ESIC for workers, public liability under the Public Liability Insurance Act for units handling hazardous substances, and boiler inspection requirements.
