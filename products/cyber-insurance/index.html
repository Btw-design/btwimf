In short

  • Two sides of cover: first-party (your own losses — restoration, business interruption, extortion, fraud) and third-party (claims and regulatory action against you).
  • Individual cyber cover reimburses losses from identity theft, unauthorised online transactions, phishing and social-media reputation harm.
  • Business cyber cover adds breach-response costs, ransomware, systems-interruption loss, data-restoration and liability to affected customers.
  • India's DPDP Act 2023 introduces penalties for mishandling personal data, sharpening the need for the liability side of cover.
  • Claims-made basis: business policies usually respond to claims made and incidents notified during the policy period — keep cover continuous.

What is Cyber Insurance in India?

Cyber insurance is a relatively new but fast-growing line. For individuals, it is a low-cost policy that steps in when you are defrauded online — money moved out of your account after a phishing link, an unauthorised card transaction, identity theft used to take a loan in your name, or a malicious impersonation on social media — reimbursing the loss and the legal and IT costs of putting things right.

For businesses, cyber insurance is broader. It funds the immediate response to an incident (forensic investigation, legal advice, customer notification, credit monitoring, PR), the cost of rebuilding data and systems, the income lost while operations are down, and any ransom or extortion payment where legally permitted. It also covers your liability if customers, partners or regulators pursue you after a breach. Coverage

What cyber insurance covers — and what it doesn’t

A quick side-by-side so you know exactly where the policy helps.

Typically covered

  • Financial loss from phishing, spoofing and fraudulent fund transfers (individuals and businesses)
  • Ransomware and cyber-extortion costs, including negotiation and, where lawful, the ransom
  • Forensic investigation, legal, notification and PR costs after an incident (breach response)
  • Data and systems restoration, and business-interruption loss while systems are down
  • Third-party liability — claims by customers or partners whose data was compromised
  • Regulatory investigation costs and, where insurable, fines and penalties
  • Identity theft resolution, and for individuals, reputational-harm and cyber-stalking legal costs

Usually excluded

  • Losses from a known vulnerability or incident that predates the policy
  • Bodily injury and physical property damage (covered by other policies)
  • Dishonest or criminal acts by the insured or senior management
  • Failure to maintain basic security standards represented in the proposal (e.g. MFA, backups)
  • War, state-sponsored 'cyber-operations' where excluded, and infrastructure/utility outages
  • Loss of future profits, goodwill and contractual penalties beyond the policy limits
  • For individuals: business or professional cyber losses (that needs a commercial policy)

Exact inclusions and exclusions vary by insurer and plan — your BTW IMF advisor walks you through the wording before you buy.

How to choose the right cyber insurance

  1. Match the cover to the risk. Work out the worst realistic loss — a long hospital stay, a total-loss claim, an income gap — and buy enough cyber insurance to absorb it.
  2. Compare like for like. Premium is only meaningful next to the sum insured, sub-limits, waiting periods and exclusions. We line these up side by side.
  3. Check the insurer, not just the plan. Claim-settlement record, turnaround time and how the insurer treats disclosure disputes matter as much as price.
  4. Add only the riders you'll use. A few add-ons sharply improve real payouts; the rest just raise the premium.

Who needs cyber insurance?

Individuals who bank, shop, trade or hold crypto online — the low premium is easily justified by a single averted fraud. Businesses of every size that hold customer data, take digital payments, rely on IT to operate, or are contractually required by clients to carry cyber cover. It is now common for enterprise customers and government tenders to demand a minimum cyber limit from their vendors.

What affects your premium

  • For individuals: the sum insured chosen (commonly ₹1–50 lakh) and cover scope
  • For businesses: annual turnover, industry, and the volume and sensitivity of data held
  • Security posture — MFA, endpoint protection, backups, patching, employee training
  • Claims and incident history
  • Policy limit, sub-limits and the deductible / waiting period for business interruption
  • Extensions selected — dependent BI, PCI fines, media liability, crime overlap

Useful add-ons & riders

Riders widen cyber insurance for a small extra premium. The ones worth considering:

  • Dependent business interruption — loss from an outage at your IT or cloud provider
  • Payment-card (PCI-DSS) fines and assessments
  • Media and content liability (defamation, IP infringement online)
  • Reputational harm / crisis-management spend beyond the base limit
  • Bricking cover — replacement of hardware rendered useless by an attack
  • Social-engineering / funds-transfer-fraud higher sub-limit
FAQs

Cyber Insurance in India — frequently asked questions

What does cyber insurance actually cover?

For individuals: money lost to phishing, spoofed calls, and unauthorised card or UPI transactions; identity theft; and the legal and IT costs of recovery. For businesses: incident-response costs (forensics, legal, notification, PR), ransomware and extortion, data and systems restoration, income lost during downtime, and liability to customers and regulators after a breach.

Do individuals need cyber insurance?

If you bank, shop, invest or hold crypto online, it is worth considering. Individual cyber cover has a low annual premium and reimburses fraud losses that banks often do not, plus the cost of restoring your identity and reputation. One prevented fraud typically pays for years of premium.

Does cyber insurance cover ransomware?

Yes. Business cyber policies cover the cost of responding to a ransomware attack — forensic investigation, negotiation, data restoration and business-interruption loss — and, where it is legally permitted and the insurer approves, the ransom payment itself. Having tested backups reduces both the loss and your premium.

Will a claim be paid if we didn't have good security?

Insurers ask about controls like multi-factor authentication, backups and patching in the proposal. If you represented that a control was in place and it was not, and that gap caused the loss, the claim can be reduced or declined. Answer the proposal accurately and keep the controls you declared.

Is cyber insurance mandatory in India?

There is no general legal mandate. However, the Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 creates significant penalties for mishandling personal data, sectoral regulators (RBI, SEBI, IRDAI) impose cyber-resilience requirements on regulated entities, and many enterprise and government contracts now require vendors to hold a minimum cyber insurance limit.

What should I do first if we are attacked?

Call the insurer's 24x7 incident-response hotline before you do anything drastic. Do not pay a ransom, wipe machines or talk publicly until the forensic and legal team advises. Preserve logs and evidence, isolate affected systems, and report financial fraud to your bank and on cybercrime.gov.in.
