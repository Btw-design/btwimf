Home Insurance in India

Home insurance pays to repair or rebuild your home and replace your belongings after events like fire, burglary, flood, earthquake or a burst pipe. You can insure the structure, the contents, or both. It is one of the cheapest policies relative to the sum insured — often a few thousand rupees a year for tens of lakhs of cover.

  • Structure and/or contents cover
  • Fire, burglary & natural disaster
  • Cover for tenants too
  • Long-term policies available

In short

  • Two parts: structure cover (rebuilding cost of the building) and contents cover (furniture, appliances, valuables).
  • Bharat Griha Raksha is IRDAI's standard home policy — a defined, easy-to-compare cover for homes with contents up to a set limit.
  • Sum insured for structure is the reconstruction cost (built-up area × construction rate), not the market price of the flat.
  • Tenants can insure their own contents even though they don't own the building.
  • Underinsurance: declaring too low a sum insured means claims are scaled down proportionately.

What is Home Insurance in India?

A home insurance policy indemnifies you for damage to your home and its contents from insured perils. The structure cover pays the cost of rebuilding or repairing the physical building; the contents cover pays to repair or replace what is inside — electronics, furniture, appliances, clothing and, within sub-limits, jewellery and valuables.

In India, the standardised Bharat Griha Raksha policy makes home cover simple to buy and compare: it automatically covers the structure and contents against a wide list of perils including fire, flood, earthquake, storm and burglary, with an in-built waiver of underinsurance up to a point. Coverage

What home insurance covers — and what it doesn’t

A quick side-by-side so you know exactly where the policy helps.

Typically covered

  • Fire, lightning, explosion and implosion
  • Natural disasters — flood, storm, cyclone, earthquake, landslide, subsidence
  • Burglary, housebreaking and theft of contents
  • Bursting or overflowing of water tanks, pipes and apparatus
  • Riot, strike, malicious damage and terrorism
  • Impact damage from vehicles, falling trees or aircraft
  • Contents in temporary accommodation and, on some plans, alternative-accommodation rent while repairs happen

Usually excluded

  • Wear and tear, gradual deterioration, rust, damp and pest damage
  • Damage to a property left unoccupied beyond a set number of days
  • Loss of cash, deeds, securities and manuscripts
  • Electrical or mechanical breakdown of appliances (unless an add-on)
  • War, nuclear risks and wilful damage by the insured
  • Structural defects and damage from unauthorised construction
  • Jewellery and valuables beyond the policy sub-limit unless separately declared

Exact inclusions and exclusions vary by insurer and plan — your BTW IMF advisor walks you through the wording before you buy.

Options

Types of home insurance

Choose based on whether you own or rent, and what you want to protect.

Structure-only cover

Insures the building against insured perils. Suits owners who have few valuable contents or whose society covers common structure.

  • Sum insured = reconstruction cost
  • Cheapest per lakh of cover
  • Add contents later if needed

Contents-only cover

Insures furniture, appliances, electronics and personal belongings. The right choice for tenants.

  • No need to own the building
  • Cover valuables with sub-limits
  • Portable if you move

Structure + contents (Bharat Griha Raksha)

The standard combined policy — building and contents against a wide peril list, with underinsurance protection built in.

  • One simple, comparable policy
  • Auto contents cover up to a limit
  • 7-year long-term option

Comprehensive home package

A broader plan adding appliance breakdown, all-risk cover for portable valuables, domestic-worker cover and personal accident.

  • Widest protection
  • Named valuables cover
  • Higher premium

How to choose the right home insurance

  1. Match the cover to the risk. Work out the worst realistic loss — a long hospital stay, a total-loss claim, an income gap — and buy enough home insurance to absorb it.
  2. Compare like for like. Premium is only meaningful next to the sum insured, sub-limits, waiting periods and exclusions. We line these up side by side.
  3. Check the insurer, not just the plan. Claim-settlement record, turnaround time and how the insurer treats disclosure disputes matter as much as price.
  4. Add only the riders you'll use. A few add-ons sharply improve real payouts; the rest just raise the premium.

Who needs home insurance?

Every homeowner — the cost of rebuilding after a fire or earthquake dwarfs the premium, and a home loan lender often requires structure cover. Tenants should insure their contents, because the landlord's policy (if any) covers only the building. Anyone in a flood-prone or seismically active area, or with valuable electronics and jewellery at home, benefits most.

What affects your premium

  • Sum insured for structure (built-up area × current construction rate per sq ft)
  • Sum insured and mix of contents, including declared valuables
  • Location — flood zone, earthquake zone, crime rate
  • Type of construction and age of the building
  • Policy term — 1 year vs long-term (up to 7 years) at a discount
  • Add-ons — appliance breakdown, all-risk jewellery, rent for alternative accommodation

Useful add-ons & riders

Riders widen home insurance for a small extra premium. The ones worth considering:

  • Electrical and mechanical appliance breakdown cover
  • All-risk cover for jewellery and portable valuables, in and away from home
  • Rent for alternative accommodation while the home is being repaired
  • Domestic-worker personal accident cover
  • Personal accident cover for the family
  • Loss of rent for landlords if an insured event makes the property uninhabitable
FAQs

Home Insurance in India — frequently asked questions

Is home insurance mandatory in India?

It is not legally mandatory for individuals, though home-loan lenders usually require at least structure (fire) cover for the loan tenure. Given how cheap it is relative to rebuilding costs, it is strongly recommended for both owners and tenants.

How is the sum insured for my house calculated?

For the structure, it is the reconstruction cost: built-up area multiplied by the current construction rate per square foot in your area (excluding land value). For contents, it is the cost to replace your belongings. Your BTW IMF advisor helps you arrive at realistic figures.

Can a tenant buy home insurance?

Yes. A tenant can and should insure their own contents — furniture, appliances, electronics and personal belongings. The building itself is the landlord's responsibility to insure.

What is Bharat Griha Raksha?

It is IRDAI's standardised home insurance product. Every insurer offers it with the same core wording, covering the structure and contents against a wide list of perils, with automatic contents cover up to 20% of the structure sum insured and protection against underinsurance. It makes home cover easy to compare.

Does home insurance cover jewellery?

Contents cover includes jewellery only up to a small percentage of the contents sum insured. To fully protect valuable jewellery, declare it separately and add an all-risk valuables cover, which also protects it outside the home.

Does it cover damage from a flood or an earthquake?

Yes. Standard Indian home policies, including Bharat Griha Raksha, cover flood, storm, cyclone, earthquake and landslide as insured perils, unlike in some other countries where these need separate cover.
