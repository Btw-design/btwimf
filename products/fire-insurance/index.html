Fire Insurance (Property Insurance)

Fire insurance (property insurance) pays to repair or replace your buildings, plant, machinery, furniture and stock if they are damaged by fire and a range of allied perils — lightning, explosion, storm, flood, earthquake, riot, and impact by vehicles or aircraft. In India it is sold through standardised products: Bharat Sookshma / Laghu Udyam Suraksha for smaller enterprises, and the Standard Fire & Special Perils policy for larger risks.

What is Fire Insurance (Property Insurance)?

A fire policy is the core property-damage cover for any physical business or valuable asset. It indemnifies you for direct physical loss or damage to the insured property from the covered perils, so you can rebuild premises, replace machinery and recover the value of destroyed stock.

Since 2021 the Indian market uses standardised wordings. Micro and small enterprises with a total sum insured up to ₹5 crore use Bharat Sookshma Udyam Suraksha; those between ₹5 crore and ₹50 crore use Bharat Laghu Udyam Suraksha. Both are broad, include many covers as standard, and waive or relax the underinsurance penalty. Risks above ₹50 crore use the Standard Fire & Special Perils policy. Fire cover is usually paired with Burglary, Machinery Breakdown, Business Interruption (loss of profit) and Marine cover for a complete programme — BTW IMF assembles the whole set.

What fire insurance covers — and what it doesn’t

A quick side-by-side so you know exactly where the policy helps.

Typically covered

  • Fire (however caused, subject to exclusions), lightning and explosion/implosion
  • Storm, cyclone, typhoon, tempest, hurricane, flood and inundation (STFI)
  • Riot, strike, malicious damage and acts of terrorism (terrorism as an add-on/pool cover)
  • Impact damage by any rail/road vehicle or animal, and by aircraft or articles dropped from them
  • Subsidence, landslide and rockslide
  • Bursting or overflowing of water tanks, apparatus and pipes; leakage from sprinklers
  • Missile testing operations and bush fire

Usually excluded

  • The first portion of each claim (the policy excess) and any amount above the sum insured
  • War, nuclear risks and pollution or contamination (unless resulting from an insured peril)
  • Wear and tear, gradual deterioration, rust, and inherent defects
  • Loss or damage to property undergoing a process where heat is applied (limited)
  • Electrical machinery damage from its own over-running or short-circuit (needs machinery cover)
  • Consequential loss such as loss of profit or rent (needs a Business Interruption policy)
  • Spontaneous fermentation, cash, and property more specifically insured elsewhere

Exact inclusions and exclusions vary by insurer and plan — your BTW IMF advisor walks you through the wording before you buy.

How to choose the right fire insurance

  1. Match the cover to the risk. Work out the worst realistic loss — a long hospital stay, a total-loss claim, an income gap — and buy enough fire insurance to absorb it.
  2. Compare like for like. Premium is only meaningful next to the sum insured, sub-limits, waiting periods and exclusions. We line these up side by side.
  3. Check the insurer, not just the plan. Claim-settlement record, turnaround time and how the insurer treats disclosure disputes matter as much as price.
  4. Add only the riders you'll use. A few add-ons sharply improve real payouts; the rest just raise the premium.

Who needs fire insurance?

Every business with physical premises, equipment or stock — manufacturers, warehouses, retailers, restaurants, hotels, hospitals, offices and workshops. Landlords insuring commercial buildings, and lenders financing property or plant (who usually make it a loan condition). Homeowners get equivalent cover through home insurance, which is built on the same fire wording.

What affects your premium

  • Total sum insured and the split between building, plant, furniture and stock
  • Construction class, occupancy (what activity happens on site) and fire-protection systems
  • Location — flood zone, seismic zone, proximity to hazardous neighbours
  • Basis of valuation — market value vs reinstatement value
  • Claims history and the excess you accept
  • Add-ons — terrorism, earthquake shock (where extra), business interruption, escalation

How a fire insurance claim works

A dedicated claim manager prepares the file and follows up with the insurer until it’s settled.

Make safe and report

Protect against further loss, call the fire brigade, and inform us / the insurer immediately. Keep the fire-brigade report and FIR where relevant.

Preserve the scene

Do not clear debris or start repairs until the surveyor has inspected, except to prevent further damage.

Document and quantify

Photographs, an inventory of damaged property with values, repair/replacement estimates, purchase invoices and stock records.

Surveyor and settlement

The insurer appoints a licensed surveyor; on agreement the claim is paid on the chosen valuation basis, less the excess and any average adjustment.

Useful add-ons & riders

Riders widen fire insurance for a small extra premium. The ones worth considering:

  • Terrorism cover (via the Indian Market Terrorism Risk Insurance Pool)
  • Business Interruption / Loss of Profit following an insured peril
  • Machinery Breakdown and Electronic Equipment Insurance
  • Escalation clause — automatic increase in sum insured through the year
  • Removal of debris beyond the standard sub-limit
  • Architects', surveyors' and consulting engineers' fees for reinstatement
FAQs

Fire Insurance (Property Insurance) — frequently asked questions

What does fire insurance cover besides fire?

Standard Indian fire policies cover a package of allied perils: lightning, explosion and implosion, storm, cyclone, flood and inundation, riot, strike and malicious damage, impact by vehicles or aircraft, subsidence and landslide, and bursting of water tanks and pipes. Terrorism is available as an add-on.

What is Bharat Sookshma Udyam Suraksha?

It is IRDAI's standardised fire and allied-perils policy for micro and small enterprises with a total sum insured up to ₹5 crore. It offers wide cover with the same wording across all insurers and waives the underinsurance ('average') penalty, making it simple and fair for small businesses. Bharat Laghu Udyam Suraksha is the equivalent for the ₹5–50 crore band.

What is the 'average clause' in fire insurance?

If you insure property for less than its full value, the average (underinsurance) clause reduces every claim in the same proportion — insure a ₹1 crore building for ₹50 lakh and a ₹20 lakh claim pays only ₹10 lakh. The Bharat Sookshma product waives this; Laghu relaxes it beyond 15% underinsurance; the Standard Fire policy applies it fully. Insure to full reinstatement value to avoid it.

Should I insure on market value or reinstatement value?

Reinstatement value (new-for-old) is strongly preferred for buildings and machinery, because it pays the cost of rebuilding or replacing with new, without deducting depreciation. Market-value cover deducts depreciation and can leave you well short of the cost to actually restore the asset.

Does fire insurance cover loss of business income after a fire?

No, not on its own. The fire policy pays for the physical damage. To cover the profit you lose and the ongoing fixed costs while you are shut down or operating at reduced capacity, you need a separate Business Interruption (Loss of Profit) policy, which we arrange alongside.

Is fire insurance mandatory?

It is not a statutory requirement for most businesses, but banks and financial institutions almost always require it as a condition of any loan secured against property, plant or stock. It is also a practical necessity given the cost of rebuilding after a serious fire.
