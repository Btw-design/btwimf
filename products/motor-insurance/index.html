A comprehensive policy adds 'own damage' cover for your own vehicle against accidents, theft, fire and natural calamities, and add-ons like zero-depreciation make claims far more useful.

In short

  • Legal minimum: a valid third-party (TP) liability policy — driving without one is a punishable offence.
  • Comprehensive = TP + own-damage + theft + fire + natural calamity + owner-driver personal accident.
  • IDV: the Insured Declared Value is your vehicle's current market value and the cap on a theft/total-loss payout.
  • NCB: a No-Claim Bonus of 20–50% off own-damage premium builds up over claim-free years and moves with you between insurers.
  • New vehicles: come bundled with 3-year (car) or 5-year (two-wheeler) third-party cover plus 1-year own-damage.

What is Motor Insurance in India?

Motor insurance is the umbrella term for car insurance, two-wheeler insurance and commercial-vehicle insurance. Every one of these must carry at least third-party liability cover under the Motor Vehicles Act — the part that protects other people, whose injury claims can run into lakhs or be unlimited for death.

A comprehensive (or 'package') motor policy also protects your own vehicle: accident damage, theft, fire, riots and natural events such as floods and cyclones. Because the base own-damage cover deducts depreciation on parts, add-ons like zero-depreciation, engine protect and roadside assistance are what turn a claim into a genuinely useful payout. Coverage

What motor insurance covers — and what it doesn’t

A quick side-by-side so you know exactly where the policy helps.

Typically covered

  • Third-party bodily injury, death and property damage you are legally liable for
  • Accidental damage to your own vehicle (comprehensive / own-damage cover)
  • Theft of the vehicle or its parts
  • Fire, explosion, self-ignition and lightning
  • Natural disasters — flood, cyclone, earthquake, landslide, storm
  • Man-made events — riot, strike, malicious damage, terrorism
  • Personal accident cover for the owner-driver (a statutory ₹15 lakh)

Usually excluded

  • Normal wear and tear, ageing, and mechanical or electrical breakdown
  • Driving without a valid licence or under the influence of alcohol or drugs
  • Using a private vehicle for hire, racing or any purpose not allowed by the policy
  • Depreciation on parts and consequential loss, unless a zero-depreciation add-on is bought
  • Damage outside the geographical area stated in the policy
  • Any loss when the policy has lapsed or the premium is unpaid
  • Contributory negligence such as driving a clearly unroadworthy vehicle

Exact inclusions and exclusions vary by insurer and plan — your BTW IMF advisor walks you through the wording before you buy.

Options

Types of motor insurance

Pick by vehicle type, age and how you use it.

Third-party liability only

The statutory minimum. Pays others for injury, death or property damage you cause; nothing for your own vehicle.

  • Cheapest, mandatory cover
  • Premium fixed by IRDAI
  • No protection for your own vehicle

Standalone own-damage

Covers only your own vehicle. Useful while the long-term third-party cover on a newer vehicle is still valid.

  • Pairs with existing TP
  • Renew every year
  • Add zero-dep on newer vehicles

Comprehensive / package

Third-party + own-damage + theft + fire + natural calamity in a single policy. The usual choice.

  • All-round protection
  • Customisable with add-ons
  • One renewal to track

Comprehensive + add-ons

A package policy loaded with zero-depreciation, engine/consumables protect, return-to-invoice and roadside assistance.

  • Full claim value, no depreciation cut
  • Best for the first 5–7 years
  • Higher premium, far better payout

How to choose the right motor insurance

  1. Match the cover to the risk. Work out the worst realistic loss — a long hospital stay, a total-loss claim, an income gap — and buy enough motor insurance to absorb it.
  2. Compare like for like. Premium is only meaningful next to the sum insured, sub-limits, waiting periods and exclusions. We line these up side by side.
  3. Check the insurer, not just the plan. Claim-settlement record, turnaround time and how the insurer treats disclosure disputes matter as much as price.
  4. Add only the riders you'll use. A few add-ons sharply improve real payouts; the rest just raise the premium.

Who needs motor insurance?

Every vehicle owner — third-party cover is compulsory and driving without it risks a fine, licence action and personal liability for someone else's medical bills. Choose comprehensive if your vehicle is under about 10 years old, financed, parked on the street, or driven in a flood-prone city, and add zero-depreciation while the vehicle is newer and spare parts are costly.

What affects your premium

  • Vehicle type, make, model, variant, cubic capacity and fuel
  • Insured Declared Value (IDV) and year of manufacture
  • City / RTO of registration — metros cost more
  • Your accumulated No-Claim Bonus and past claims
  • Add-ons chosen — zero-dep, engine protect, RTI, consumables, roadside assistance
  • Voluntary deductible and any anti-theft device discount

Useful add-ons & riders

Riders widen motor insurance for a small extra premium. The ones worth considering:

  • Zero-depreciation — full part cost with no depreciation deducted at claim time
  • Engine and gearbox protection — water ingress, oil leakage and consequential damage
  • Return to invoice — pays the original invoice value on total loss or theft
  • No-Claim Bonus protection — keeps your NCB even after one claim
  • Consumables cover — engine oil, nuts, bolts and lubricants in a claim
  • Roadside assistance — towing, flat tyre, battery, fuel and lockout help
FAQs

Motor Insurance in India — frequently asked questions

Is motor insurance mandatory in India?

Yes. Under the Motor Vehicles Act every motor vehicle must have at least a valid third-party liability policy. Driving without it can mean a fine of ₹2,000 (first offence) or more, possible imprisonment, and personal liability for any injury or damage you cause.

What is the difference between third-party and comprehensive motor insurance?

Third-party covers only your legal liability to other people and their property. Comprehensive covers that plus damage to your own vehicle from accidents, theft, fire and natural disasters. Comprehensive costs more but is the only cover that protects your own car or bike.

Does one motor policy cover both my car and my bike?

No. Each vehicle needs its own policy. If you own several vehicles, BTW IMF can align the renewal dates and manage them together so nothing lapses.

What is IDV and why does it matter?

IDV (Insured Declared Value) is your vehicle's current market value after depreciation and the maximum you receive if it is stolen or written off. Setting it too low to save premium means a smaller payout when you most need it.

Will I lose my No-Claim Bonus if I switch insurers?

No. The NCB belongs to you, not the insurer. When you move, the new insurer applies your existing NCB on production of the previous policy or a renewal notice showing it.

Can I renew a motor policy that has already expired?

Yes, but the vehicle usually has to be inspected before a lapsed policy is renewed, and if it has been expired for more than 90 days you lose your accumulated NCB. Renew before expiry to avoid both.
