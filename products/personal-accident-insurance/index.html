Personal Accident Insurance

Personal accident (PA) insurance pays a fixed lump sum if an accident causes your death or permanent disability, and a weekly income if it leaves you temporarily unable to work. Cover is 24x7 and worldwide, the premium is among the lowest of any policy, and it fills a gap that life and health insurance leave — lost earning capacity after an accident.

  • Accidental death & disability
  • 24x7 worldwide cover
  • Weekly income benefit
  • Very low premium

In short

  • What it pays: 100% of the sum insured on accidental death or permanent total disability; a percentage for permanent partial disability; a weekly amount for temporary total disability.
  • Sum insured: usually allowed up to 10–20 times your annual income.
  • Cost: typically a few hundred to a couple of thousand rupees a year for ₹25 lakh–₹1 crore of cover.
  • Extras often included: ambulance charges, hospital cash, children's education benefit and funeral expenses.
  • Standalone vs rider: a standalone PA policy usually gives wider disability definitions and higher limits than a rider.

What is Personal Accident Insurance?

A personal accident policy is a benefit policy: it pays pre-agreed amounts on the occurrence of defined accidental events, regardless of the actual expense. If an accident causes death or the loss of both limbs or eyesight, it pays the full sum insured; if it causes the loss of one limb, it pays a set percentage; if it puts you in bed rest for weeks, it pays a weekly benefit while you recover.

PA cover is valuable because life insurance pays only on death and health insurance pays only hospital bills — neither replaces the income you lose while recovering from an accident or living with a disability. It is also inexpensive. BTW IMF helps you set a sum insured linked to your income and choose between an individual plan, a family plan and a rider on your health or term policy.

Coverage

What personal accident cover covers — and what it doesn’t

A quick side-by-side so you know exactly where the policy helps.

Typically covered

  • Accidental death — 100% of the sum insured to the nominee
  • Permanent total disability (e.g. loss of both limbs, both eyes, or speech and hearing) — 100–125%
  • Permanent partial disability — a fixed percentage per the policy schedule
  • Temporary total disability — a weekly benefit for a limited number of weeks
  • Accidental hospitalisation, ambulance charges and hospital daily cash (on most plans)
  • Add-on benefits — children's education fund, loan protector, funeral expenses
  • Worldwide, 24x7 cover including on the road, at work and at home

Usually excluded

  • Death or disability from illness or natural causes (that is life/health insurance)
  • Suicide, self-inflicted injury and being under the influence of alcohol or drugs
  • Injuries from adventure sports, aviation (other than as a passenger) and professional racing unless covered
  • Pre-existing disability and any consequence of a condition that existed before the policy
  • War, riots where excluded, and breach of law with criminal intent
  • Pregnancy, childbirth and their complications

Exact inclusions and exclusions vary by insurer and plan — your BTW IMF advisor walks you through the wording before you buy.

Options

Types of personal accident cover

Choose by who you want to cover and how wide the disability definition should be.

Individual PA plan

Covers one person for death, disability and weekly benefit. The simplest and most common option.

  • Own sum insured
  • Wide disability schedule
  • Add hospital cash and education fund

Family PA plan

One policy covering the earning member fully and spouse/children for accidental death and medical expenses at lower limits.

  • Covers the whole family
  • Child cover for education
  • Cost-effective bundle

PA rider on a term / health plan

An accidental-death or accidental-disability rider attached to your life or health policy.

  • Very low cost
  • Fewer disability categories
  • Ends with the base policy

Group PA (employer)

Cover arranged by an employer for its workforce, often required under labour rules for certain roles.

  • Covers all employees
  • Meets statutory duty-of-care
  • Low per-head cost

How to choose the right personal accident cover

  1. Match the cover to the risk. Work out the worst realistic loss — a long hospital stay, a total-loss claim, an income gap — and buy enough personal accident cover to absorb it.
  2. Compare like for like. Premium is only meaningful next to the sum insured, sub-limits, waiting periods and exclusions. We line these up side by side.
  3. Check the insurer, not just the plan. Claim-settlement record, turnaround time and how the insurer treats disclosure disputes matter as much as price.
  4. Add only the riders you'll use. A few add-ons sharply improve real payouts; the rest just raise the premium.

Who needs personal accident cover?

Every earning person, and especially anyone who travels a lot by road, works in a physically risky job, rides a two-wheeler, or is the sole earner for a family. It is also a sensible, cheap top-up for people who already have life and health cover, because it addresses disability and lost income specifically. Government schemes like PMSBY provide a small base cover; a private PA plan scales it to your real income.

What affects your premium

  • Your occupation risk class (desk job vs field/manual work)
  • Sum insured chosen, linked to your annual income
  • Scope — death only, or death plus permanent and temporary disability
  • Add-ons — hospital cash, children's education, EMI/loan protector, funeral expenses
  • Individual vs family vs group cover
  • Age, though PA premiums are far less age-sensitive than life or health

Riders widen personal accident cover for a small extra premium. The ones worth considering:

  • Hospital daily cash during accidental admission
  • Children's education benefit on death or permanent total disability
  • Loan / EMI protector to clear or service debt
  • Broken-bones benefit and burns benefit

FAQs

Personal Accident Insurance — frequently asked questions

What is the difference between personal accident insurance and life insurance?

Life insurance pays only on death, from any cause. Personal accident insurance pays on death or disability caused specifically by an accident, and also pays a weekly income during temporary disability. PA cover is much cheaper and is meant to complement, not replace, a life policy.

Does personal accident cover disability, not just death?

Yes — that is its main strength. It pays the full sum insured for permanent total disability, a scheduled percentage for permanent partial disability (such as loss of a finger or an eye), and a weekly benefit while you are temporarily unable to work.

How much personal accident cover should I take?

Link it to your income: roughly 10–15 times your annual earnings, plus outstanding loans. Because the premium is so low, there is little reason to under-insure.

Is a PA rider on my term plan enough?

A rider is a cheap way to add accidental-death cover, but standalone PA policies usually offer wider disability definitions, higher limits, weekly income benefits and extras like children's education. Most people benefit from a standalone plan.

Does personal accident insurance cover COVID or illness?

No. PA cover responds only to injury caused by a sudden, external, visible and violent accidental event. Illness of any kind, including COVID, is covered by health insurance, not PA.

Is the payout taxable?

Personal accident insurance benefits are generally treated as tax-free capital receipts in the hands of the nominee or the insured. Premiums for a standalone PA policy are not eligible for the Section 80D health-insurance deduction.
