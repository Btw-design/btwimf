  • New cars: come with a 3-year third-party + 1-year own-damage package; renew own-damage yearly.

What is car insurance in India?

A car insurance policy is a yearly contract that pays for defined losses involving your vehicle. Every car on an Indian road must have at least third-party insurance, which pays for injury, death or property damage you cause to someone else — liability that can run into lakhs or more.

A comprehensive (or 'package') policy also covers your own car against accident damage, theft, fire, riots and natural events like floods. Because the base own-damage cover deducts depreciation on parts, add-ons such as zero-depreciation, engine protect and roadside assistance are what make a claim genuinely worthwhile. Coverage

What car insurance covers — and what it doesn't

A quick side-by-side so you know exactly where the policy helps.

Typically covered

  • Third-party injury, death and property damage you are liable for (mandatory)
  • Accidental damage to your own car (comprehensive)
  • Theft of the vehicle
  • Fire, explosion and self-ignition
  • Natural disasters — flood, cyclone, earthquake, landslide
  • Riots, strikes, vandalism and terrorism
  • Personal accident cover for the owner-driver (mandatory ₹15 lakh)

Usually excluded

  • Normal wear and tear, ageing and mechanical/electrical breakdown
  • Damage when driving without a valid licence or under the influence
  • Damage while using a private car for commercial hire or racing
  • Consequential loss and depreciation on parts (unless zero-dep is added)
  • Driving outside the geographical limits or in a restricted area
  • Tyres and tubes except when damaged in the same accident (limited)
  • Any loss when the policy has lapsed

Exact inclusions and exclusions vary by insurer and plan — your BTW IMF adviser walks you through the wording before you buy.

How to choose the right car insurance

  1. Match the cover to the risk. A newer, financed or street-parked car in a flood-prone city needs comprehensive with zero-dep; an old runabout may be fine on third-party plus basic own-damage.
  2. Compare like for like. Premium is only meaningful next to the IDV, deductible, add-ons and exclusions. We line these up side by side.
  3. Check the insurer, not just the plan. Claim-settlement record, garage network and turnaround time matter as much as price.
  4. Add only the riders you'll use. Zero-dep, engine protect and NCB protection sharply improve real payouts; the rest just raise the premium.

Who needs car insurance?

Every car owner — third-party cover is compulsory. Choose comprehensive if your car is less than about 10 years old, financed, parked on the street, or driven in a flood-prone city. Add zero-depreciation for the first 5–7 years and whenever spare parts are expensive. Older, low-value cars may be fine on third-party plus a basic own-damage cover.

What affects your premium

  • Insured Declared Value (IDV) — higher IDV, higher own-damage premium
  • Make, model, variant, engine cc and fuel type
  • City of registration (metro RTOs cost more) and year of manufacture
  • Your No-Claim Bonus — up to 50% off own-damage after 5 claim-free years
  • Add-ons selected — zero-dep, engine protect, RTI, consumables, RSA
  • Voluntary deductible and any past claims history

FAQs

Car insurance in India — frequently asked questions

Is car insurance mandatory in India?

Yes. Under the Motor Vehicles Act, every car must have at least a valid third-party liability policy. Driving without it can mean a fine, and you would personally have to pay for any injury or damage you cause to others.

What is the difference between third-party and comprehensive car insurance?

Third-party covers only your legal liability to other people and their property. Comprehensive covers that plus damage to your own car from accidents, theft, fire and natural disasters. Comprehensive costs more but protects your own vehicle.

What is IDV in car insurance?

IDV (Insured Declared Value) is the current market value of your car after depreciation. It is the maximum amount you receive if the car is stolen or written off. A higher IDV means a slightly higher premium but a bigger payout — don't under-declare it to save premium.

What is a No-Claim Bonus (NCB)?

NCB is a discount on your own-damage premium for every claim-free year — 20% after year one, rising to 50% after five years. One claim resets it to zero, unless you have an NCB-protection add-on. You keep your NCB when you switch insurers.

Is zero-depreciation cover worth it?

For cars up to about 5–7 years old, yes. Without it, the insurer deducts depreciation on plastic, rubber and metal parts at claim time, so you pay a large share of a bumper or panel replacement yourself. Zero-dep removes that deduction.

Can I renew a car policy that has already expired?

Yes, but the car must usually be inspected before a lapsed policy is renewed, and you lose your accumulated NCB if it has been expired for more than 90 days. Renew before expiry to avoid both.
