Life Insurance in India

Life insurance pays an agreed sum of money to your nominee if you die during the policy term. Its core job is to replace your income and clear your loans so your family's lifestyle and goals stay on track. Pure-protection term insurance gives the largest cover for the lowest premium; savings and market-linked plans combine a smaller cover with an investment.

  • Income replacement for your family
  • Cover from ₹1 crore upwards
  • Section 80C tax benefit
  • Tax-free payout under Sec 10(10D)

In short

  • What it does: pays a lump sum (or income) to your nominee on death during the term.
  • How much cover: a common rule of thumb is 10–15× your annual income, plus outstanding loans.
  • Cheapest form: term insurance — pure cover, no maturity value.
  • Tax: premiums deductible under Section 80C; death benefit generally tax-free under Section 10(10D).
  • Buy early: premiums are locked at your entry age for the whole term.

What is Life Insurance in India?

Life insurance is a contract where you pay premiums and the insurer promises to pay a defined 'sum assured' to your family if you die while the policy is in force. Some plans also pay a maturity amount if you survive the term.

There are three broad families: term plans (protection only), traditional savings plans such as endowment and money-back (protection plus a guaranteed but modest return), and ULIPs (protection plus a market-linked investment). BTW IMF's advisors explain the trade-offs honestly — for most families, a large term plan plus separate investments (mutual funds, PPF) beats bundling the two.

Coverage

What life insurance covers — and what it doesn’t

A quick side-by-side so you know exactly where the policy helps.

Typically covered

  • Death of the life assured during the policy term (any cause, after the initial exclusions)
  • Terminal illness — many term plans advance the payout on diagnosis
  • Accidental death — an extra sum if that rider is added
  • Critical illness — a lump sum on diagnosis if that rider is added
  • Waiver of premium — future premiums waived on disability or critical illness (rider)
  • Maturity benefit — on endowment, money-back and ULIP plans if you survive the term

Usually excluded

  • Suicide within the first 12 months of the policy or a revival
  • Death due to non-disclosure or fraud in the proposal (e.g. hidden illness or smoking)
  • Death while participating in hazardous activities excluded by the policy
  • Claims when the policy has lapsed for non-payment of premium
  • Pre-existing critical illnesses within the rider's waiting period
  • Death due to involvement in criminal activity or war/terrorism where excluded

Exact inclusions and exclusions vary by insurer and plan — your BTW IMF advisor walks you through the wording before you buy.

Options

Types of life insurance

The main product types and who each suits.

Term insurance

Pure protection: a large cover for a low premium, with nothing paid back if you survive (unless you choose return-of-premium).

  • Highest cover per rupee
  • Ideal primary policy
  • Level premium for the whole term

Endowment / money-back

Life cover plus a guaranteed lump sum or periodic payouts. Lower returns but disciplined, low-risk saving.

  • Guaranteed maturity value
  • Conservative returns
  • Good for risk-averse savers

ULIP

Life cover plus a market-linked investment across equity/debt funds, with tax advantages and a 5-year lock-in.

  • Market-linked growth
  • Switch funds tax-free
  • Charges front-loaded — hold long term

Whole life

Cover that lasts to age 99/100, useful for estate planning and leaving a guaranteed legacy.

  • Lifelong cover
  • Estate/legacy planning
  • Higher premium

Retirement / annuity

Converts a corpus into a guaranteed lifelong income after retirement.

  • Pension for life
  • Removes longevity risk
  • Annuity income is taxable

How to choose the right life insurance

  1. Match the cover to the risk. Work out the worst realistic loss — a long hospital stay, a total-loss claim, an income gap — and buy enough life insurance to absorb it.
  2. Compare like for like. Premium is only meaningful next to the sum insured, sub-limits, waiting periods and exclusions. We line these up side by side.
  3. Check the insurer, not just the plan. Claim-settlement record, turnaround time and how the insurer treats disclosure disputes matter as much as price.
  4. Add only the riders you'll use. A few add-ons sharply improve real payouts; the rest just raise the premium.

Who needs life insurance?

Anyone whose income supports other people — a spouse, children, dependent parents — or anyone with a home loan or business debt. If no one would suffer financially when you die, you may not need life cover yet. Homemakers who manage the household and single people with a large loan or co-signed debt should also consider it.

What affects your premium

  • Your age at entry — lock in cover young
  • Sum assured and policy term
  • Smoking / tobacco use — typically 30–50% higher premium
  • Health, BMI, family medical history and occupation
  • Plan type — term is cheapest, ULIP/endowment cost more for the same cover
  • Riders added — accidental death, critical illness, waiver of premium

Useful add-ons & riders

Riders widen life insurance for a small extra premium. The ones worth considering:

  • Accidental death benefit — an extra sum assured if death is accidental
  • Critical illness rider — lump sum on diagnosis of a listed illness
  • Waiver of premium — future premiums waived on disability or critical illness
  • Accidental total & permanent disability — payout if an accident ends your ability to earn
  • Income benefit — pays the family a monthly amount in addition to the lump sum
FAQs

Life Insurance in India — frequently asked questions

How much life insurance cover do I need?

A practical starting point is 10–15 times your annual income, plus all outstanding loans, minus existing savings and cover. So someone earning ₹12 lakh a year with a ₹40 lakh home loan might target around ₹1.6–2 crore. Your BTW IMF advisor refines this to your goals.

Which is better — term insurance or an endowment/ULIP plan?

For protection, term insurance is the most efficient: it gives the largest cover for the lowest premium. Endowment and ULIP plans bundle a smaller cover with an investment and cost far more per rupee of protection. Most families are better served by a big term plan plus separate investments.

Is the life insurance payout taxable?

The death benefit is generally exempt from income tax under Section 10(10D), subject to the conditions in force. Premiums paid are deductible under Section 80C within the overall limit.

What happens if I stop paying premiums?

A term plan lapses and cover ends (there is usually a 15–30 day grace period and a revival window of up to 5 years). Traditional plans may acquire a reduced 'paid-up' value after a few years' premiums.

Will the claim be rejected if I had a health condition?

Not if you disclosed it. Claims are rejected mainly for non-disclosure or misstatement — hidden illnesses, understated income, or not declaring tobacco use. Declare everything; the insurer prices it in and the claim is then secure.

Can I buy life insurance if I smoke or have a medical condition?

Yes. Smokers pay a higher premium; many conditions are accepted with a loading or specific exclusion. We place the case with an insurer likely to offer standard or near-standard terms.
