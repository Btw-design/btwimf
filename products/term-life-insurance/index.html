Term Life Insurance

Term Life Insurance

Term life insurance is pure-protection life cover: you pay a fixed annual premium and, if you die during the term, your nominee receives the full sum assured. There is no maturity payout if you survive (unless you choose return-of-premium). It gives the most cover for the lowest cost — often ₹1 crore for a healthy 30-year-old at a modest monthly premium.

  • ₹1 crore+ cover
  • Lowest premium life cover
  • Level premium for the full term
  • Payout is tax-free

In short

  • What it does: pays the sum assured to your nominee on death during the term; nothing if you outlive it.
  • Premium: fixed at your entry age and locked for the whole term — buy young.
  • Term to choose: up to the age you expect to stop earning or dependents to become independent (often 60–65).
  • Tax: Section 80C on premiums, Section 10(10D) on the death benefit (subject to conditions).
  • Options: lump sum, monthly income, increasing cover, or return-of-premium.

What is Term Life Insurance?

A term plan is the simplest life insurance product. You pick a sum assured (say ₹1 crore) and a term (say 30 years), pay level premiums, and the insurer pays your family that sum if you die within the term. Because there is no savings element, almost your entire premium buys protection — which is why the cover is so large for the money.

Modern term plans add flexibility: level or increasing cover, payout as a lump sum and/or monthly income, the ability to increase cover at marriage or childbirth, and riders for accidental death, critical illness and premium waiver. Coverage

What term insurance covers — and what it doesn’t

A quick side-by-side so you know exactly where the policy helps.

Typically covered

  • Death due to any cause during the policy term, after the initial exclusions
  • Terminal illness — most plans pay the sum assured early on diagnosis
  • Accidental death — an additional amount if that rider is added
  • Critical illness — a lump sum on diagnosis if that rider is added
  • Waiver of future premiums on permanent disability or critical illness (rider)
  • Return of all premiums on survival — only if you buy the return-of-premium variant

Usually excluded

  • Suicide within the first 12 months of the policy or its revival
  • Death from a condition or habit you did not disclose (e.g. smoking, existing illness)
  • Death during excluded hazardous pursuits or, in some plans, while under the influence
  • Claims after the policy has lapsed for unpaid premium (outside the grace/revival period)
  • Critical-illness rider claims within the rider waiting period
  • Any maturity or survival benefit on a standard (non-ROP) term plan

Exact inclusions and exclusions vary by insurer and plan — your BTW IMF advisor walks you through the wording before you buy.

Options

Types of term insurance

The variants worth knowing about.

Level term plan

A fixed sum assured for the whole term at a level premium. The standard, most cost-effective choice.

  • Simplest and cheapest
  • Cover stays constant
  • Ideal primary policy

Increasing cover plan

The sum assured rises each year (e.g. 5–10%) to keep pace with inflation and a growing family.

  • Cover grows over time
  • Premium set upfront
  • Good for young earners

Income / income-plus-lump-sum

Pays part as a lump sum and the rest as monthly income for 10–20 years, helping families manage money.

  • Replaces a salary
  • Reduces misuse risk
  • Flexible split

Return of premium (TROP)

Refunds all premiums paid if you survive the term. Higher premium — the 'extra' is effectively a low-return saving.

  • Money back on survival
  • 2–3× the cost of plain term
  • Consider plain term + investing the difference

How to choose the right term insurance

  1. Match the cover to the risk. Work out the worst realistic loss — a long hospital stay, a total-loss claim, an income gap — and buy enough term insurance to absorb it.
  2. Compare like for like. Premium is only meaningful next to the sum insured, sub-limits, waiting periods and exclusions. We line these up side by side.
  3. Check the insurer, not just the plan. Claim-settlement record, turnaround time and how the insurer treats disclosure disputes matter as much as price.
  4. Add only the riders you'll use. A few add-ons sharply improve real payouts; the rest just raise the premium.

Who needs term insurance?

Every earning person with dependents or debt: a spouse or children who rely on your income, dependent parents, a home or business loan, or a loan you have co-signed. The younger and healthier you are, the cheaper the lifelong premium. Non-earning spouses who run the household, and single people with large liabilities, should also consider a plan.

What affects your premium

  • Entry age — the dominant factor; premiums roughly double every 8–10 years of delay
  • Sum assured and policy term
  • Tobacco/nicotine use — a materially higher 'smoker' rate
  • Health, BMI, family history and occupation
  • Annual income — insurers cap cover at a multiple of income
  • Riders and payout options chosen (ROP, increasing cover, income)

Useful add-ons & riders

Riders widen term insurance for a small extra premium. The ones worth considering:

  • Accidental death benefit — extra cover if death is by accident
  • Accidental total & permanent disability — payout if an accident ends your earning ability
  • Critical illness rider — lump sum on diagnosis of a listed illness
  • Waiver of premium — future premiums waived on disability or critical illness
  • Child support / income benefit — a regular payout to the family on top of the lump sum
FAQs

Term Life Insurance — frequently asked questions

What is the difference between term insurance and life insurance?

Term insurance is a type of life insurance — the pure-protection type. 'Life insurance' also includes endowment, money-back and ULIP plans that add a savings or investment element. Term gives the biggest cover for the lowest premium and pays only on death within the term.

What term length should I choose?

Choose a term that runs until you expect to stop earning or your dependents become financially independent — commonly to age 60 or 65. A longer term costs a little more but removes the risk of being uninsurable later.

Do I get money back at the end of a term plan?

Not with a standard term plan — that is why it is cheap. A return-of-premium (TROP) variant refunds your premiums on survival, but costs 2–3 times more; investing the difference usually leaves you better off.

Why do smokers pay more for term insurance?

Tobacco use raises mortality risk, so insurers apply a 'smoker' premium that can be 30–50% higher. You must declare it — a claim can be denied if undisclosed tobacco use is found later, even years afterwards.

Can my family claim if I die abroad?

Yes. Term plans cover death worldwide. The nominee provides the local death certificate (translated and attested) along with the usual documents; we help coordinate this.

How much cover can I get?

Insurers cap the sum assured at a multiple of your annual income (often 15–25× for younger applicants) and consider your net worth. ₹1–2 crore is common for salaried professionals; higher covers need income and financial proof.
