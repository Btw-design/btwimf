What is Commercial Insurance?

A commercial package (sometimes called an 'office package' or 'business package') policy lets a business insure its premises and operations under one contract with several optional sections. You choose the sections that apply — a paperless consultancy might take only electronic equipment, money and public liability; a showroom would add fire, burglary and plate glass.

The advantage over separate policies is simplicity: one proposal, one premium, one renewal date and one claims contact, usually at a lower combined cost. The property section typically uses the standardised Bharat Sookshma or Laghu Udyam Suraksha wording, so cover is broad and comparable across insurers. Coverage

What commercial insurance covers — and what it doesn’t

A quick side-by-side so you know exactly where the policy helps.

Typically covered

  • Building, fit-outs, furniture, equipment and stock against fire and allied perils (storm, flood, riot, impact, etc.)
  • Burglary, housebreaking and theft following forcible entry
  • Money in the till, in the safe and in transit to and from the bank
  • Electronic equipment — computers, servers, POS, medical and office electronics — including breakdown
  • Plate and fixed glass, and neon or illuminated signs
  • Fidelity guarantee — loss from dishonesty of named employees
  • Public liability — third-party injury or property damage on your premises

Usually excluded

  • Wear and tear, gradual deterioration and maintenance
  • Money loss from unattended premises or unexplained shortages
  • Data and software (covered only for reinstatement of media, not lost information — see cyber)
  • Employee injury claims (needs workmen's compensation / group personal accident)
  • Professional negligence and advice-based claims (needs professional indemnity)
  • Damage from a peril not selected (e.g. earthquake or terrorism if excluded)
  • Any amount above the section sum insured or sub-limit

Exact inclusions and exclusions vary by insurer and plan — your BTW IMF advisor walks you through the wording before you buy.

Types of commercial insurance

The package is modular — take the sections your business actually needs.

Office essentials

Fire on contents and fit-outs, electronic equipment, money and public liability. The core for a services business.

  • No building cover needed if leased
  • Covers laptops and servers
  • Light proposal

Showroom / trading package

Adds building (if owned), stock, burglary and plate glass to the essentials — protection for a customer-facing premises.

  • Stock and display cover
  • Glass and signage
  • Burglary with forcible entry

Package + business interruption

Any of the above plus loss-of-income cover for the time the premises are unusable after a claim.

  • Covers profit and fixed costs
  • Set an adequate indemnity period
  • Priced on gross profit

Multi-location package

One policy scheduling several branches or offices, with a floating stock/contents sum insured where useful.

  • One renewal for all sites
  • Consistent wording
  • Central claims contact

How to choose the right commercial insurance

  1. Match the cover to the risk. Work out the worst realistic loss — a long hospital stay, a total-loss claim, an income gap — and buy enough commercial insurance to absorb it.
  2. Compare like for like. Premium is only meaningful next to the sum insured, sub-limits, waiting periods and exclusions. We line these up side by side.
  3. Check the insurer, not just the plan. Claim-settlement record, turnaround time and how the insurer treats disclosure disputes matter as much as price.
  4. Add only the riders you'll use. A few add-ons sharply improve real payouts; the rest just raise the premium.

Who needs commercial insurance?

Offices, professional practices, clinics, showrooms, agencies, IT and service firms, and small traders — any commercial premises that isn't a factory or heavy industrial site. Landlords of commercial property, and tenants required by their lease to insure contents and take public liability, also use it.

What affects your premium

  • Sections selected and their sums insured / sub-limits
  • Nature of the business and footfall (public liability rating)
  • Construction, occupancy and fire/security protection of the premises
  • Location — flood zone, crime rate, neighbouring risks
  • Money limits (safe, till, transit) and safe specification
  • Claims history and the policy excess

FAQs

Commercial Insurance — frequently asked questions

What is the difference between commercial insurance and business insurance?

'Business insurance' is the umbrella term for a company's whole insurance programme across all lines. 'Commercial insurance' usually refers specifically to a package policy for premises and operations — property, burglary, money, equipment and public liability bundled together. Commercial cover is one part of a full business programme.

Can I choose only some sections of a commercial package?

Yes. The package is modular. A paperless consultancy might take only electronic equipment, money and public liability, while a showroom would add fire, stock, burglary and plate glass. You pay only for the sections you select.

Does commercial insurance cover my stock?

Yes, if you include the stock section. Stock is usually insured at actual (market) value rather than reinstatement value, and you should raise the sum insured during peak stocking periods such as festival season.

Is public liability included?

Public liability is one of the standard sections and covers third-party injury or property damage on your premises — a customer slipping, or damage to a neighbouring unit. Businesses with higher footfall or product risk often increase the limit or add product liability.

What is the money section?

It covers cash and negotiable instruments against loss — money in a locked safe, money in the till during business hours (within a lower limit), and money in transit between the premises and the bank. Cover usually requires the safe to meet a minimum specification.

Do I still need cyber insurance if I have a commercial package?

Yes. The electronic equipment section covers the physical hardware and the cost of reinstating media, but not the loss of data, a ransomware demand, fund-transfer fraud or your liability to customers after a breach. Those need a separate cyber policy.
