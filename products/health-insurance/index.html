What is Health Insurance in India?

Health insurance is a contract between you and an insurer: you pay a yearly premium, and the insurer pays for covered medical treatment up to an agreed limit called the sum insured. Most claims today are cashless — the hospital bills the insurer directly at a network hospital, so you don't pay upfront.

In India, health plans are regulated by the IRDAI. They can be bought for an individual, as a family floater (one shared sum insured for the whole family), or as a group plan through an employer. BTW IMF is an IRDAI-licensed Insurance Marketing Firm, which means we can advise on and place policies from multiple insurers rather than sell just one brand.

In short

  • What it does: reimburses or directly settles hospital bills up to your sum insured.
  • Typical sum insured: ₹5 lakh–₹1 crore for individuals and families.
  • Waiting periods: ~30 days initial, 2–4 years for pre-existing diseases, 9 months–4 years for specific illnesses and maternity.
  • Tax: premiums qualify for deduction under Section 80D of the Income Tax Act.
  • Best bought: young and healthy, when premiums are lowest and waiting periods start early.

Coverage

What health insurance covers — and what it doesn’t

Switch between the two to see exactly where the policy helps.

  • In-patient hospitalisation (room, ICU, doctor and surgeon fees, medicines)
  • Pre-hospitalisation (typically 30–60 days) and post-hospitalisation (60–90 days) expenses
  • Day-care procedures that don't need 24-hour admission (e.g. cataract, dialysis)
  • Ambulance charges up to a specified limit
  • Annual preventive health check-up
  • AYUSH (Ayurveda, Yoga, Unani, Siddha, Homeopathy) treatment at recognised hospitals
  • Modern treatments and, on many plans, mental-health hospitalisation
  • Pre-existing diseases during the waiting period (usually the first 2–4 years)
  • Cosmetic or aesthetic procedures unless medically necessary
  • OPD, dental and vision unless a rider is added
  • Treatment for self-inflicted injury or substance abuse
  • Non-medical / consumable items unless a 'consumables' cover is added
  • Hospitalisation purely for evaluation, rest cure or rehabilitation
  • Claims arising from non-disclosure or wrong information in the proposal

Exact inclusions and exclusions vary by insurer and plan — your BTW IMF advisor walks you through the wording before you buy.

Options

Types of health insurance

The five common structures and who each one suits best.

01

Individual plan

A separate sum insured for one person. Best for single earners or anyone who wants their own dedicated cover.

  • Own sum insured
  • Premium based on your age
  • No sharing with family
02

Family floater

One shared sum insured for the whole family. Usually the most cost-effective way to cover a young family.

  • Covers spouse, children, parents
  • Lower combined premium
  • One renewal to track
03

Senior citizen plan

Designed for members above 60, with cover for age-related conditions and shorter waiting periods on some plans.

  • Higher entry age
  • Pre-existing cover sooner on some plans
  • Co-payment may apply
04

Top-up / super top-up

A low-cost plan that sits above a deductible — ideal for boosting a base or employer policy affordably.

  • Large cover at low premium
  • Kicks in above a threshold
  • Pairs with a base plan
05

Critical illness plan

Pays a lump sum on diagnosis of a listed illness such as cancer or heart attack, separate from hospital bills.

  • Fixed payout
  • Use for any expense
  • Complements a regular plan

How to choose the right health insurance

1
Match the cover to the risk.

Work out the worst realistic loss — a long hospital stay, a total-loss claim, an income gap — and buy enough health insurance to absorb it.

2
Compare like for like.

Premium is only meaningful next to the sum insured, sub-limits, waiting periods and exclusions. We line these up side by side.

3
Check the insurer, not just the plan.

Claim-settlement record, turnaround time and how the insurer treats disclosure disputes matter as much as price.

4
Add only the riders you'll use.

A few add-ons sharply improve real payouts; the rest just raise the premium.

Who needs it, and what it costs

Who needs it

Who needs health insurance?

Everyone — but especially anyone without an employer plan, families with dependent children or ageing parents, self-employed professionals, and people planning a family (maternity has a 9-month–4-year wait). Even if your employer covers you, a personal plan protects you between jobs and after retirement, when buying fresh cover is harder and costlier.

Premium factors

What affects your premium

  • Age of the eldest insured member — the single biggest factor
  • Sum insured and the city you live in (metro vs non-metro)
  • Family size and whether parents are included
  • Pre-existing conditions and lifestyle (smoking, BMI)
  • Add-ons chosen — room-rent waiver, consumables, OPD, maternity
  • Any voluntary co-payment or deductible you accept in return for a lower premium

Claims

How a health insurance claim works

A dedicated claim manager prepares the file and follows up with the insurer until it’s settled.

1

Intimate the claim

Call us or the insurer's helpline before a planned admission, or within 24 hours of an emergency.

2

Cashless or reimbursement

For cashless, the network hospital's insurance desk sends the pre-authorisation. For non-network, pay and claim later.

3

Submit documents

Discharge summary, bills, reports and the claim form. Your BTW IMF claim manager checks the file before it goes in.

4

Settlement

The insurer approves and pays the hospital (cashless) or credits your account (reimbursement), usually within 7–15 days.

Add-ons

Useful add-ons & riders

Riders widen your health insurance for a small extra premium. The ones worth considering:

Room-rent waiver

Removes the cap on your hospital room category, so a claim isn’t reduced for choosing a higher room tier.

Consumables cover

Pays for gloves, syringes and similar non-medical items that a base policy often excludes.

Maternity & newborn cover

For planned pregnancies — buy well in advance, as it carries its own waiting period.

OPD & doctor consultation

Covers clinic visits, medicines and diagnostics outside a hospital admission.

Personal accident rider

Pays a lump sum on accidental death or disability, on top of your health cover.

Hospital cash

A fixed daily allowance for each day of admission, paid regardless of the actual bill.

FAQs

Health Insurance in India — frequently asked questions

Is health insurance mandatory in India?

No, health insurance is not legally mandatory for individuals. It is strongly recommended because a single hospitalisation can cost several lakh rupees, and premiums are far cheaper when you are young and healthy.

What is a cashless claim?

A cashless claim means the insurer settles your bill directly with a network hospital, so you don't pay upfront (except for non-covered items and any co-payment). You show your health card, the hospital sends a pre-authorisation request, and treatment proceeds once it is approved.

How much health cover do I need?

As a general guide, aim for a sum insured of at least ₹10 lakh in a metro city, or ₹5 lakh in a smaller town, per adult. A top-up plan is a cheap way to raise this to ₹25–50 lakh.

What is a pre-existing disease waiting period?

It is the time you must hold the policy before conditions you already had at purchase (such as diabetes or hypertension) are covered. It is typically 2–4 years, and it resets if you let the policy lapse, so renew on time.

Can I cover my parents on the same policy?

Yes, either by adding them to a family floater or, more commonly, with a separate senior-citizen plan. A separate plan usually works out better because premiums rise with the eldest member's age.

Does health insurance give a tax benefit?

Yes. Under Section 80D you can deduct the premium paid for yourself and family, plus an additional amount for parents, subject to the limits in force for the year. Preventive check-up costs count within these limits.
