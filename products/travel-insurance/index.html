What is Travel Insurance?

Travel insurance is a short-term policy that runs for the length of a trip (or a year, for frequent flyers). Its core purpose is to pay for a medical emergency overseas — a hospital stay in Europe or the US can cost tens of lakhs of rupees — including emergency evacuation and repatriation.

Beyond medical, it reimburses non-refundable costs if you must cancel or cut short a trip for covered reasons, compensates you for long flight delays and missed connections, and covers checked baggage that is lost, delayed or damaged. Student and senior-citizen variants exist. BTW IMF matches the plan to your destination, trip length, age and any visa rules.

Coverage

What travel insurance covers — and what it doesn’t

A quick side-by-side so you know exactly where the policy helps.

Typically covered

  • Emergency medical expenses, hospitalisation and dental pain relief abroad
  • Emergency medical evacuation and repatriation of remains
  • Trip cancellation and curtailment for covered reasons (illness, family emergency)
  • Flight delay, missed connection and trip interruption compensation
  • Checked baggage loss, delay and damage
  • Loss of passport and emergency travel-document assistance
  • Personal liability abroad and, on some plans, adventure-sport cover as an add-on

Usually excluded

  • Pre-existing medical conditions unless specifically declared and accepted
  • Treatment you travelled specifically to obtain (medical tourism)
  • Injuries from adventure or extreme sports unless an add-on is purchased
  • Incidents while under the influence of alcohol or drugs
  • Losses from war, civil unrest or travel against government advisory
  • Cancellation for reasons not listed in the policy (e.g. change of mind)
  • Baggage left unattended, and cash, jewellery or electronics beyond sub-limits

Exact inclusions and exclusions vary by insurer and plan — your BTW IMF advisor walks you through the wording before you buy.

Options

Types of travel insurance

Pick by how often and where you travel.

Single-trip plan

Covers one return journey for its exact duration. The default for a holiday or one-off business trip.

  • Priced per trip
  • Choose the medical sum insured
  • Add-ons for adventure sports

Multi-trip / annual plan

Covers unlimited trips in a year, each up to a set number of days (e.g. 30 or 45). Best for frequent flyers.

  • One policy all year
  • Per-trip day cap
  • Cheaper than repeat single trips

Student travel plan

For students studying abroad — longer duration, cover for study interruption, sponsor protection and mental-health support.

  • Semester/annual terms
  • Meets university requirements
  • Compassionate visit cover

Senior citizen plan

For travellers above 60–70, with higher medical limits and pre-existing-disease cover options.

  • Higher age eligibility
  • PED cover add-on
  • May need a medical declaration

How to choose the right travel insurance

  1. Match the cover to the risk. Work out the worst realistic loss — a long hospital stay, a total-loss claim, an income gap — and buy enough travel insurance to absorb it.
  2. Compare like for like. Premium is only meaningful next to the sum insured, sub-limits, waiting periods and exclusions. We line these up side by side.
  3. Check the insurer, not just the plan. Claim-settlement record, turnaround time and how the insurer treats disclosure disputes matter as much as price.
  4. Add only the riders you'll use. A few add-ons sharply improve real payouts; the rest just raise the premium.

Who needs travel insurance?

Anyone travelling abroad — it is compulsory for Schengen and many student, work and tourist visas, and strongly advisable everywhere else because overseas healthcare is expensive and not covered by your Indian health plan. Domestic travel insurance is optional but useful for trip delays, baggage and personal-accident cover on long or expensive itineraries.

What affects your premium

  • Destination — the US and Canada cost more than Asia or Europe
  • Trip duration and the traveller's age (over-60 premiums rise steeply)
  • Medical sum insured chosen (US$50,000 to US$500,000+)
  • Single-trip vs annual multi-trip
  • Pre-existing-disease cover and adventure-sport add-ons
  • Number of travellers — family and group plans offer a discount

FAQs

Travel Insurance — frequently asked questions

Is travel insurance mandatory?

It is mandatory for the Schengen visa (minimum €30,000 medical cover) and for many student, work and visitor visas to other countries. Where it is not compulsory it is still strongly recommended, because a medical emergency abroad can cost more than the entire trip.

Does travel insurance cover Covid-19?

Most current plans cover Covid-19 as any other illness — emergency treatment and hospitalisation abroad, and often quarantine costs if you test positive during the trip. Confirm the specific wording, which we check before you buy.

How much medical cover do I need for a trip?

For Schengen, at least €30,000 (about US$50,000). For the US, Canada or a long trip, US$100,000–500,000 is sensible given hospital costs there. For Asia, US$50,000–100,000 is usually adequate.

Can I buy travel insurance after starting my trip?

Generally no — cover must begin on or before your departure from India. A few insurers offer limited 'already abroad' plans with a waiting period, but they are restrictive. Buy before you fly.

Will it cover a pre-existing condition?

Only if you declare it and the insurer accepts it, usually for a 'life-threatening emergency' of a stable condition and often with an extra premium. An undeclared pre-existing condition can lead to a rejected medical claim.

What should I do if I need medical help abroad?

Call the insurer's 24x7 assistance number first (it is on your policy certificate). They will direct you to a suitable hospital and arrange cashless treatment or issue a payment guarantee, and coordinate evacuation if needed.
