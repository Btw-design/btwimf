Family Health Insurance

Family health insurance — usually a 'family floater' — covers every member of your family under a single sum insured and a single premium. Any member, or several members, can use the cover in a policy year up to the total limit. It is typically the most cost-effective way to insure a young family together.

  • One premium, whole family
  • Shared sum insured
  • Add newborns mid-term
  • Cashless hospitalisation

In short

  • Who's covered: self, spouse, dependent children, and often parents/in-laws.
  • How it works: one shared limit — e.g. ₹10 lakh — that any member can draw on.
  • Premium driver: the age of the oldest member on the policy.
  • Restore benefit: many floaters reinstate the sum insured if it runs out during the year.
  • Tip: keep elderly parents on a separate plan to avoid inflating the family premium.

What is Family Health Insurance?

A family floater is a single health policy that spreads one sum insured across several people. If your cover is ₹10 lakh and one member has an ₹6 lakh claim, ₹4 lakh remains for the rest of the year (unless a restore benefit tops it back up).

Floaters are priced on the eldest member's age, so they are excellent value while everyone is young, and less efficient once a parent in their 60s is added. BTW IMF models both options — one floater vs floater-plus-senior-plan — and shows you the real numbers.

Coverage

What family health insurance covers — and what it doesn’t

A quick side-by-side so you know exactly where the policy helps.

Typically covered

  • Hospitalisation for any covered member up to the shared sum insured
  • Pre- and post-hospitalisation expenses for each member
  • Day-care procedures and modern treatments
  • Newborn baby cover, usually from day one after a waiting period
  • Annual health check-up for adults
  • Ambulance charges and AYUSH treatment
  • Restore / recharge of the sum insured on many plans

Usually excluded

  • Pre-existing diseases of any member during the waiting period
  • Maternity unless a maternity benefit is included and its waiting period is over
  • OPD, dental and spectacles unless added as a rider
  • Cosmetic treatment and non-medical consumables (unless covered)
  • Treatment outside India unless the plan has global cover
  • Claims from undisclosed conditions of any insured member

Exact inclusions and exclusions vary by insurer and plan — your BTW IMF advisor walks you through the wording before you buy.

Options

Types of family health insurance

How families usually structure their cover.

Self + spouse + kids floater

The classic young-family plan — one sum insured, lowest combined cost.

  • Priced on the older spouse's age
  • Kids covered cheaply
  • Simple single renewal

Floater + separate senior plan

Parents on their own senior-citizen policy so their age doesn't raise everyone's premium.

  • Keeps the core floater cheap
  • Parents get age-appropriate cover
  • Two policies to renew

Floater + super top-up

A modest base floater plus a large, low-cost top-up for big-ticket claims.

  • High total protection
  • Low overall premium
  • Top-up shared by the family too

Multi-individual plan

Separate sums insured for each member inside one policy document — useful when members' needs differ a lot.

  • Dedicated cover per person
  • One policy, one renewal
  • Slightly higher premium

How to choose the right family health insurance

  1. Match the cover to the risk. Work out the worst realistic loss — a long hospital stay, a total-loss claim, an income gap — and buy enough family health insurance to absorb it.
  2. Compare like for like. Premium is only meaningful next to the sum insured, sub-limits, waiting periods and exclusions. We line these up side by side.
  3. Check the insurer, not just the plan. Claim-settlement record, turnaround time and how the insurer treats disclosure disputes matter as much as price.
  4. Add only the riders you'll use. A few add-ons sharply improve real payouts; the rest just raise the premium.

Who needs family health insurance?

Any household with two or more members to insure — especially couples starting out, families with children, and anyone currently relying only on an employer plan that won't follow them between jobs. If your parents are over 60, ask us to compare adding them to the floater vs a dedicated senior plan.

What affects your premium

  • Age of the eldest member on the policy
  • Number of members and whether parents are included
  • Sum insured and your city
  • Pre-existing conditions declared for any member
  • Add-ons — maternity, room-rent waiver, consumables
  • Voluntary co-payment or deductible

Useful add-ons & riders

Riders widen family health insurance for a small extra premium. The ones worth considering:

  • Maternity and newborn cover — plan 9–48 months ahead of a pregnancy
  • Room-rent waiver — no cap on room category for any member
  • Unlimited restore — reinstates the sum insured more than once a year
  • Consumables cover — pays for non-medical items in the bill
  • OPD cover — clinic visits and medicines for the family
FAQs

Family Health Insurance — frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a family floater and individual plans?

A family floater gives one shared sum insured for everyone at a lower combined premium, priced on the eldest member's age. Individual plans give each person their own dedicated cover at a higher total cost. Young families usually prefer a floater; families with an elderly member often split into a floater plus a separate senior plan.

Can I add my newborn baby to the policy?

Yes. Most family floaters let you add a newborn from a set number of days after birth (often 90) by an endorsement, and many cover the baby from day one if a newborn-cover benefit is included. Tell us within the notification window.

Whose age decides the premium?

The age of the oldest person on the policy. This is why adding a 65-year-old parent can sharply increase the premium for the whole family, and why a separate senior-citizen plan is often cheaper overall.

What happens if the whole sum insured is used up?

Without a restore benefit, no further claims are payable that policy year. Many modern floaters include a 'restore' or 'recharge' that reinstates the full sum insured for unrelated future claims in the same year.

Should I keep my employer's family cover and also buy my own?

Yes, ideally. An employer plan ends when you leave the job and often has low limits or co-pay. A personal floater is portable, builds up your pre-existing-disease waiting period, and stays with you after retirement.

Is there a tax benefit on a family health plan?

Yes, the premium qualifies for a deduction under Section 80D for self, spouse and children, with an additional limit for parents. The exact caps depend on the members' ages and the year's rules.
