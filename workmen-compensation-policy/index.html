Workmen Compensation Policy (Employees’ Compensation Insurance)

A Workmen Compensation policy pays the compensation an employer is legally obliged to pay a worker — or their dependants — when the worker is injured, disabled, contracts an occupational disease, or dies because of their job. It covers the employer's statutory liability under the Employees' Compensation Act 1923 (formerly the Workmen's Compensation Act) and the associated legal and medical costs.

In short

  • Who it protects: the employer — against claims from employees or their dependants for work-related injury, occupational disease or death.
  • Legal basis: the Employees' Compensation Act 1923 (renamed from the Workmen's Compensation Act), which makes the employer liable regardless of fault.
  • Compensation formula: fixed by the Act from the worker's monthly wages and age, with defined amounts for death and for permanent total, permanent partial and temporary disability.
  • Not the same as ESIC: employees earning up to the ESI wage limit are covered by ESIC instead; a WC policy covers those outside ESI and contractors' / project labour.
  • Different from Group Personal Accident: GPA is a no-fault benefit owned by the employee; a WC policy indemnifies the employer's legal liability.

What is Workmen Compensation Policy (Employees’ Compensation Insurance)?

Under the Employees' Compensation Act, an employer must pay compensation when a worker suffers personal injury by accident 'arising out of and in the course of' their employment, or contracts a listed occupational disease. The liability applies whether or not the employer was negligent, and the amount is set by a statutory formula based on the worker's wages and age.

A Workmen Compensation (WC) policy transfers that liability to an insurer. It pays the statutory compensation, medical expenses (within limits), and the legal costs of defending or settling a claim before the Commissioner for Employees' Compensation. It is standard for factories, workshops, construction and infrastructure projects, logistics and any business employing manual or field workers — and is frequently required by principal employers, tender authorities and lenders. BTW IMF sets the wage roll and headcount correctly, adds the medical and common-law extensions where relevant, and manages claims through the process.

Coverage

What workmen compensation cover covers — and what it doesn’t

A quick side-by-side so you know exactly where the policy helps.

Typically covered

  • Compensation payable under the Employees' Compensation Act for a worker's death
  • Compensation for permanent total, permanent partial and temporary disablement
  • Occupational diseases listed in Schedule III of the Act contracted through the employment
  • Medical, surgical and hospital expenses arising from a covered injury (with an add-on / sub-limit)
  • Legal costs and expenses of defending or settling a claim, incurred with the insurer's consent
  • Liability to contract labour and sub-contractors' workers, where declared and endorsed
  • Optional extension for common-law liability (claims in tort beyond the Act's scale)

Usually excluded

  • Any liability the employer assumes by agreement that is beyond their liability under the Act
  • Injury from war, invasion, civil war and nuclear risks
  • Injury or disease caused by the worker being under the influence of alcohol or drugs
  • Deliberate self-injury, or wilful disobedience of a documented safety rule or removal of a safety device
  • Employees not disclosed in the wage roll / declaration to the insurer
  • Injuries not resulting in death or disablement of more than three days
  • Fines and penalties imposed on the employer, and any sum above the policy limits

Exact inclusions and exclusions vary by insurer and plan — your BTW IMF advisor walks you through the wording before you buy.

Options

Types of workmen compensation cover

The policy has two tables of cover; most employers take Table A.

Table A — full Act cover

Covers the employer's liability under the Employees' Compensation Act, at common law, and (in some states) under the Fatal Accidents Act. The standard, compliant choice.

  • Meets the statutory obligation
  • Includes common-law liability
  • Legal costs covered

Table B — common law only

Covers only liability at common law and under the Fatal Accidents Act, not the Employees' Compensation Act. Rarely appropriate.

  • Narrower cover
  • Lower premium
  • Does not satisfy the Act on its own

Project / contract labour WC

A policy covering the workforce on a specific construction or erection project, aligned to the project period and often required in joint names.

  • Tied to the project duration
  • Covers sub-contract labour
  • Tender and principal-employer compliant

WC + medical extension

Table A cover plus reimbursement of the injured worker's actual medical expenses up to a chosen limit per person / per event.

  • Adds medical expense cover
  • Reduces out-of-pocket cost
  • Set a realistic per-person limit

How to choose the right workmen compensation cover

  1. Match the cover to the risk. Work out the worst realistic loss — a long hospital stay, a total-loss claim, an income gap — and buy enough workmen compensation cover to absorb it.
  2. Compare like for like. Premium is only meaningful next to the sum insured, sub-limits, waiting periods and exclusions. We line these up side by side.
  3. Check the insurer, not just the plan. Claim-settlement record, turnaround time and how the insurer treats disclosure disputes matter as much as price.
  4. Add only the riders you'll use. A few add-ons sharply improve real payouts; the rest just raise the premium.

Who needs workmen compensation cover?

Any employer of manual, skilled or field workers who is liable under the Employees' Compensation Act and is not covered by ESIC for those workers: factories, workshops, construction and infrastructure contractors, warehousing and logistics firms, plantations, mines, transport operators, security and facility-management companies, and manpower / staffing agencies. Principal employers routinely require their contractors to hold a WC policy, and it is a standard condition in government and private tenders.

What affects your premium

  • Total annual wages (wage roll) of the workers to be covered
  • Number of employees and their occupation / risk category
  • Nature of the business and the hazard level of the work
  • Table A vs Table B, and whether a medical-expenses extension is added
  • Claims experience of the business
  • For projects — the project value, duration and use of sub-contract labour

The insurer assesses the statutory compensation; where required the matter is filed before the Commissioner for Employees' Compensation. We prepare and represent the employer's position.

The insurer pays the statutory compensation (deposited with the Commissioner for a death or major disability claim) and the covered medical and legal costs.

FAQs

Workmen Compensation Policy (Employees’ Compensation Insurance) — frequently asked questions

What is a Workmen Compensation policy?

It is an employer's liability insurance that covers the compensation an employer must legally pay under the Employees' Compensation Act 1923 when a worker is injured, disabled, contracts an occupational disease, or dies as a result of their employment. It also covers the medical and legal costs of handling such a claim.

Is Workmen Compensation insurance mandatory in India?

The liability to pay compensation is mandatory under the Employees' Compensation Act — buying insurance to cover it is not strictly compulsory by that Act, but it is required in practice: the Building & Other Construction Workers Act and many state rules effectively require it, principal employers demand it from contractors, and it is a standard tender and contract condition. Without it, the employer pays every claim directly.

What is the difference between Workmen Compensation and ESIC?

ESIC is a contributory state scheme covering employees earning up to the ESI wage ceiling for medical care, sickness, maternity and employment-injury benefits. A Workmen Compensation policy covers the employer's liability for employees who are outside ESI (higher earners, and workers in areas or establishments where ESI does not apply) and for contract and project labour. Many employers need both.

How is the compensation amount calculated?

The Employees' Compensation Act fixes it by formula from the worker's monthly wages and age. For death and permanent total disablement it is a multiple of a portion of monthly wages by an age-based factor (subject to a wage cap for the calculation); permanent partial disablement pays a percentage of that based on the loss of earning capacity; temporary disablement pays a half-monthly instalment during the disability.

Does the policy cover contract labourers?

Yes, if they are declared. A principal employer or contractor can extend the policy to cover the employees of sub-contractors by endorsement and by including their wages in the declaration. This is essential on construction and infrastructure projects, where most of the workforce is contract labour.

What is the difference between this and a Group Personal Accident policy?

A Group Personal Accident (GPA) policy pays a fixed benefit to the employee or nominee for accidental death or disability, on a no-fault basis, 24x7, and the benefit belongs to the employee. A Workmen Compensation policy indemnifies the employer against a legal claim under the Act, for work-related injury only. Employers often carry both — GPA as a welfare benefit and WC for statutory compliance.
