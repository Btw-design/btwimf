Why renewing on time matters. A lapsed policy pays nothing — and re-buying costs you. Health cover restarts pre-existing-disease waiting periods; motor cover loses your No-Claim Bonus and needs a re-inspection; life cover has to be revived, sometimes with fresh medicals. A reminder is all it takes to avoid every one of these.

Before you renew

What to check, by policy type

Renewal is the one moment each year to make sure the cover still matches your life.

Health & family

Is the sum insured still enough for today’s hospital costs? Consider a top-up. Check any accumulated no-claim bonus, and whether it’s time to add maternity or reduce a co-payment.

Motor — car & bike

Renew before the expiry date to keep your No-Claim Bonus and avoid a re-inspection. Re-check the IDV against your car’s current value and keep zero-depreciation while the car is newer.

Life & term

Pay within the grace period so cover never lapses. Revisit whether the cover amount still matches your income and loans, and keep the nominee details current.

Home, travel & business

Update home contents and business stock/turnover figures for inflation and new purchases. For frequent travellers, an annual multi-trip plan often beats renewing single-trip cover each time.

Grace periods & key renewal rules

The windows and consequences differ by policy type — the essentials:

Renew-by rules at a glance

  • Health insurance: a grace period of 15–30 days applies for renewal; miss it and pre-existing-disease waiting periods and continuity benefits can reset.
  • Motor insurance: there is no grace period. Renew on or before the expiry date — a lapse of more than 90 days forfeits your accumulated No-Claim Bonus and usually forces a vehicle inspection.
  • Life & term: a grace period of 15–30 days, then the policy lapses; it can normally be revived within 5 years, sometimes with fresh medicals.
  • Switching insurer (portability): for health cover you must apply to port at least 45 days before your renewal date to carry your waiting-period credit across.
  • Any policy: a claim made while the policy is lapsed is not payable — even for an event during the earlier period, if intimation is after expiry.

FAQs

Renewals — frequently asked questions

Can BTW IMF renew a policy I bought somewhere else?

In many cases yes — for most health and motor policies we can take over servicing at renewal and manage it going forward at no cost to you. Bring your latest policy copy and we’ll check. Life and some group policies are harder to move mid-term.

What happens if my policy has already lapsed?

It depends on the type and how long ago. Health and motor policies within the grace/short window can often be renewed after a fresh declaration or inspection; a longer lapse usually means a new policy (and losing continuity). Life policies can be revived within 5 years. Contact us quickly — the sooner, the more we can save.

Will my premium go up at renewal?

It can, for reasons outside anyone’s control — your age, medical inflation, your claims in the year, or a change in the insurer’s rates. We check the renewal notice against the current market and tell you honestly whether it’s fair or worth moving.

Can I change my sum insured or add-ons at renewal?

Yes — renewal is the natural point to increase the sum insured, add or drop riders, add a family member, or attach a top-up plan. We review this with you before confirming.

Can I switch to a different insurer at renewal?

Yes. Health insurance is portable: your waiting-period credit moves with you if you apply at least 45 days before renewal. Motor cover can be switched freely while keeping your No-Claim Bonus. We handle the paperwork either way.

Do you charge for renewal assistance?

No. Renewal tracking, reminders, market checks and processing are part of the service for every policy BTW IMF holds for you. You pay the same premium as renewing direct.

How early should I renew?

Aim to confirm your renewal 1–2 weeks before expiry — earlier if you want to compare insurers or port your health cover (45 days). We’ll prompt you in good time.
