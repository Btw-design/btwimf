Why a SIP

Small amounts, invested with discipline

A SIP takes the two hardest parts of investing — starting, and staying consistent — and automates them.

You average your cost

Because the same amount is invested every month, you buy more units when prices are low and fewer when they're high — so you don't need to guess market tops and bottoms.

Compounding does the work

Returns earn their own returns. The growth portion of the chart starts small and then pulls away from your invested amount — most of it in the later years.

It fits a salary

You can start with as little as ₹500–₹1,000 a month and step the amount up each year as your income grows.

It stays flexible

Pause, increase, reduce or stop instalments any time. Redeem partially or fully when you reach the goal — there's no lock-in for open-ended funds (ELSS excepted).

Under the hood

How this calculator works

It uses the standard future-value-of-a-series formula, compounded monthly.

FV = P × ( [ (1 + i)n − 1 ] ÷ i ) × (1 + i)

  • P — your monthly investment (the "Monthly investment" slider)
  • i — monthly rate of return = (annual return ÷ 12) ÷ 100
  • n — number of monthly instalments = investment period in years × 12

Invested amount = P × n  ·  Estimated returns = FV − Invested amount  ·  Projected corpus = FV

The instalment is assumed to be paid at the start of each month and the rate of return is assumed to stay constant for the whole period. Everything runs in your browser — no inputs are sent anywhere or saved.

Read this before you rely on the number

What the estimate does not include

Market ups and downs

Real returns are never a straight line. A constant rate hides the sequence risk of a bad few years near the end, when your corpus is largest.

Tax on redemption

Long-term capital gains on equity mutual funds are taxed at 12.5% above the annual exemption; debt fund gains are taxed at your slab rate. The calculator shows a pre-tax figure.

Inflation

₹1 crore in 20 years will not buy what ₹1 crore buys today. For goal planning, discount the target back to today's money or raise your target.

Step-ups & lump sums

The projection assumes a flat monthly amount for the entire period. Increasing the SIP annually, or adding bonuses as lump sums, changes the outcome significantly.

FAQs

SIP calculator — frequently asked questions

What is a SIP?

A Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) is a way of investing a fixed amount into a mutual fund scheme at a regular interval, usually monthly. The amount is auto-debited from your bank account and buys units at whatever the price is on that date, so you average your cost over market ups and downs instead of trying to time the market.

How accurate is this SIP calculator?

It is an illustration, not a forecast. It assumes a single, constant annual rate of return for the whole period and that every instalment is invested on time. Real mutual fund returns vary year to year and can be negative in some years, so your actual corpus will differ from the figure shown.

What rate of return should I enter?

As a rough guide, diversified equity funds in India have delivered around 11–12% a year over long periods, hybrid funds less, and debt funds around 6–8% — none of it guaranteed. Use a conservative number, then check the result again with a lower rate to see the downside. An advisor can help you set a realistic assumption for your fund mix.

Does the calculator account for inflation, taxes and fund charges?

No. It shows a gross projection. The scheme's expense ratio is already reflected in the fund's returns, and on redemption you may pay capital gains tax (for equity funds, 12.5% on long-term gains above the annual exemption). Inflation also reduces what the final corpus can buy. Treat the figure as a before-tax, before-inflation estimate.

Can I increase or pause my SIP later?

Yes. Most schemes let you step up the SIP amount each year, add lump sums, pause instalments for a few months, or stop altogether without penalty. Stepping up the amount as your income grows has a large effect on the final corpus.
