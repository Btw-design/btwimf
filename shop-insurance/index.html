Shop Insurance in India

Shop insurance is a single package policy that protects a retail business — its premises, stock, fixtures, cash and equipment — against fire, burglary, natural disasters and third-party claims. Most Indian shops are covered under the standardised Bharat Sookshma Udyam Suraksha policy (for total values up to ₹5 crore), often with a business-interruption section for lost income while the shop is shut.

What is Shop Insurance in India?

A shop's entire livelihood sits in one small space — the stock on the shelves, the till, the billing system, the fit-out and the footfall. A fire next door, a monsoon flood, a break-in, or a customer injured by a falling display can each wipe out months of earnings. Shop insurance transfers those risks for a modest annual premium.

Rather than buying fire, burglary, money and liability cover separately, a retailer takes one shopkeeper's package. In India this is now usually the Bharat Sookshma Udyam Suraksha policy for the property side (identical wording across insurers, and no penalty for modest underinsurance), with additional sections for money, electronic equipment, plate glass, fidelity and public liability, plus an optional business-interruption cover. Coverage

What shop insurance covers — and what it doesn’t

A quick side-by-side so you know exactly where the policy helps.

Typically covered

  • Fire, lightning, explosion, and allied perils — storm, flood, inundation, riot, strike, malicious damage, impact
  • Burglary, housebreaking and theft following forcible and violent entry to the shop
  • Stock, furniture, fixtures, fittings and the shop fit-out / building
  • Money in the till during business hours, in a locked safe, and in transit to and from the bank
  • Electronic equipment — billing systems, POS terminals, CCTV, computers — including breakdown
  • Plate and fixed glass, mirrors, and neon or illuminated signboards
  • Public liability for injury to customers or damage to their property on the premises; fidelity guarantee for employee dishonesty

Usually excluded

  • Wear and tear, gradual deterioration, and stock spoilage from natural causes or refrigeration failure (unless added)
  • Money losses from an unattended shop or unexplained cash shortages
  • Theft without forcible entry (e.g. shoplifting during business hours) unless a specific extension is taken
  • Goods held in trust or on consignment unless declared
  • Damage from a peril not selected, e.g. earthquake or terrorism where excluded
  • Loss of licences, goodwill, and any amount above a section's sum insured or sub-limit
  • Employee injury claims (needs group personal accident / workmen's compensation)

Exact inclusions and exclusions vary by insurer and plan — your BTW IMF advisor walks you through the wording before you buy.

How to choose the right shop insurance

  1. Match the cover to the risk. Work out the worst realistic loss — a long hospital stay, a total-loss claim, an income gap — and buy enough shop insurance to absorb it.
  2. Compare like for like. Premium is only meaningful next to the sum insured, sub-limits, waiting periods and exclusions. We line these up side by side.
  3. Check the insurer, not just the plan. Claim-settlement record, turnaround time and how the insurer treats disclosure disputes matter as much as price.
  4. Add only the riders you'll use. A few add-ons sharply improve real payouts; the rest just raise the premium.

Who needs shop insurance?

Every retail business with a physical outlet — grocery and kirana stores, apparel and footwear shops, electronics and mobile retailers, jewellers (with additional specific cover), pharmacies, bakeries, salons, hardware and general stores. Shop owners who lease their premises still need cover for stock, fit-out, equipment and liability; those who own the building add the structure. Landlords of shop units insure the building and often require the tenant to carry public liability.

What affects your premium

  • Total sum insured across stock, fit-out, equipment and (if owned) building
  • Trade — what you sell, and any fire or theft-attractive stock
  • Construction, location, and fire/security protection (shutters, CCTV, alarm, safe)
  • Money limits selected for till, safe and transit
  • Whether business interruption is included and the indemnity period chosen
  • Claims history and the policy excess

Useful add-ons & riders

Riders widen shop insurance for a small extra premium. The ones worth considering:

  • Business interruption / loss of profit
  • Stock deterioration cover for refrigeration or power failure (perishables)
  • Shoplifting / theft without forcible entry extension
  • Portable equipment cover away from the premises
  • Higher public-liability limit for high-footfall stores
  • Terrorism cover for the property section
FAQs

Shop Insurance in India — frequently asked questions

What does shop insurance cover?

A shop insurance package covers your premises or fit-out, stock, furniture and fixtures against fire, burglary and natural perils; cash in the till, safe and in transit; electronic billing and CCTV equipment; plate glass and signage; employee dishonesty (fidelity); and public liability for injury to customers on the premises. Loss of income while the shop is shut can be added as a business-interruption section.

Which policy do most shops in India use?

Most shops use Bharat Sookshma Udyam Suraksha for the property side — IRDAI's standardised policy for enterprises with a total sum insured up to ₹5 crore. It has identical wording across all insurers, covers a wide range of perils, and does not penalise modest underinsurance. Money, glass, equipment and liability are added as further sections.

Does shop insurance cover theft by shoplifters?

Not under the standard burglary section, which requires forcible and violent entry — so it covers a break-in, not shoplifting during business hours. Some insurers offer a shoplifting or 'theft without forcible entry' extension for an additional premium; retailers of small high-value items should consider it.

How should I value my stock for the policy?

Insure stock at its cost or market value, and set the sum insured to reflect your highest stock levels, not the average — raise it before festival, wedding or sale seasons when you hold much more inventory. Underinsuring stock means every claim is scaled down.

Do I need shop insurance if I rent my premises?

Yes. The landlord's insurance, if any, covers only the building structure. Your stock, fit-out, equipment, cash and your public liability to customers are your responsibility, and a fire or burglary would fall entirely on you without cover.

What is the business interruption section?

It replaces the income your shop loses while it cannot trade — or trades at reduced capacity — after an insured event such as a fire or flood, and covers the fixed costs (rent, salaries, loan EMIs) you still have to pay. It is set based on your gross profit and an indemnity period long enough to refit the shop and rebuild footfall.
